The Hungary White Card is the route most often described as Hungary's digital nomad visa, but it is narrower than many remote workers expect.

## Short answer

The White Card may fit a third-country national who works remotely for a foreign employer or foreign business, has sufficient foreign income, and does not pursue gainful activity in Hungary or hold a share in a Hungarian company.

- Remote work must be connected to a foreign employer or foreign business
- Hungarian employment generally does not fit the White Card
- The official income signal is EUR 3,000 net monthly income for at least 6 months before entry
- Accommodation, insurance, passport, and remote-work proof are usually important

## Who this route fits

The White Card can fit remote employees, contractors, and foreign-company owners when the work is performed digitally and the economic activity is outside Hungary. It is not a shortcut for taking a Hungarian job.

## Income and document proof

The official factsheet refers to at least EUR 3,000 net monthly legal income for at least 6 months before entry. Applicants should prepare bank statements, contracts, employer confirmations, invoices, tax records, or company documents depending on the income type.

## Common mismatch cases

White Card may not fit if the applicant works for a Hungarian company, has Hungarian clients as the main income source, owns a Hungarian company, or plans to run a local Hungarian business. Those cases may need employment or self-employment review.

## FAQ

### Can I work for a Hungarian company with a White Card?

The White Card is not designed for Hungarian gainful activity. If the work is for a Hungarian employer or business, another residence route should be reviewed.

### Is the White Card a freelancer visa?

Not exactly. Some freelancers may fit if their work and income are foreign, but local business activity in Hungary can point away from the White Card.

### Can family members join on the White Card?

Family planning should be reviewed separately because family options depend on sponsor status, route type, and current rules.
